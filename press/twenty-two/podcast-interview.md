## 🎙️ TRANSCRIPT

### COLD OPEN (0:00 - 0:30)

**[Cue music — Razor instrumental intro, fades down]**

**ALEX:** This is KCRW's *Morning Becomes Eclectic*. I'm Alex Henderson. Today on the show — a band you've maybe heard about, maybe not yet, but you will. Pyro Altar. Four kids from Pacifica, California, who just recorded their debut album in six weeks and came out the other side with something that sounds like nothing else released this year. Twelve songs. Twelve memories. One year. *Twenty-Two.* The singer is Cole Sterling. Cole, welcome.

**COLE:** Thanks for having me, Alex.

**ALEX:** I want to start before the music. Before the album. Who was Cole Sterling at fifteen?

### ACT 1: BEGINNINGS (0:30 - 2:30)

**COLE:** At fifteen, I was a kid in Pacifica who taught himself three chords on a guitar my mother bought at a yard sale for nine dollars. The guitar had a paint-chipped stratocaster body and a broken high-E. I didn't know what I was doing. I just knew that when I played those three chords in my mother's living room, the room felt different.

**ALEX:** Three chords. Which three?

**COLE:** G, C, D. Eventually an Em. That's the whole album. Track 1, "Razor," opens with G, C, D, Em. That's the four chords I knew when I was fifteen. The album ends with the same four chords. Five different ways, but the same four chords.

**ALEX:** And the album is about that period. Fifteen to twenty-two.

**COLE:** Yeah. The seven years where you become the person who eventually does whatever you're going to do. For me, that's seven years of trying to play in a band.

**ALEX:** Trying.

**COLE:** Trying. We're still trying.

### ACT 2: THE JOURNEY (2:30 - 5:30)

**ALEX:** The album is structured chronologically. Track 1 is the first guitar. Track 12 is the moment after the tour ends and you come home.

**COLE:** Yeah. The album is in order. It's a story about a year, but the year is the year a kid became a band, and then became someone else.

**ALEX:** Walk me through a couple of tracks. Track 5 — "Twenty-Two" — the title track. What is that song about?

**COLE:** It's about the week after the tour ended. I went back to my mother's house. I picked up the first guitar I ever owned. The paint-chipped stratocaster. The broken high-E. Same guitar. And I started writing.

**ALEX:** What did you write?

**COLE:** I wrote about the things that came before the band. The first show. The first van. The first dive bar. The first person I loved. My mother. The younger version of myself who never heard of any of this. The kid who was just sitting in his mother's living room with three chords and a dream.

**ALEX:** And the realization?

**COLE:** The realization that I didn't know who I was without the band. That the kid I used to be was gone. That the kid had been gone for years. That the kid was the person I'd been pretending to be.

**ALEX:** That's a heavy thing to write down at twenty-two.

**COLE:** It's a heavy thing to realize at twenty-two. The song is just the writing down.

**ALEX:** Track 9 — "Freeway." It's about the first tour.

**COLE:** Yeah. Driving all night. The van broke down in Wyoming at 4 AM. That moment where you stand on the side of the I-80 with the engine smoking and you realize this is what you signed up for. You signed up for the breakdowns. You signed up for the couch surfing. You signed up for the thing that doesn't stop.

**ALEX:** And the song is loud.

**COLE:** The song is loud. The song is the loudest moment on the album. Because that moment is loud. The moment where the van breaks down is loud. The whole world is loud in that moment.

### ACT 3: THE BAND (5:30 - 7:30)

**ALEX:** Let's talk about the band. River, Mace, Ash. You met them how?

**COLE:** River I met at a house show in 2017. He was playing electric guitar in a punk band and I was in the audience and I knew — I knew — that was the guy. I didn't talk to him that night. I waited three weeks and then I asked him to come over and play guitar and he said yes.

**ALEX:** And Mace?

**COLE:** Mace responded to a flyer I put up at the local music store. "Bassist wanted. Four-piece. We play in a garage." He came over. He had a bass he'd owned for two years and had never played. He learned four songs in two weeks. He was in.

**ALEX:** Ash?

**COLE:** Ash was the last piece. We had three guitar players and a singer and no drummer. We didn't know any drummers. Ash's sister was dating River's cousin. Small town. We asked if she knew any drummers. She said her brother played drums. We asked him to come over. He came over. He sat down. He played along with us for an hour. He didn't say anything. At the end he said "I'm in." He's been in ever since.

**ALEX:** That's a beautiful band story.

**COLE:** It's a Pacifica band story.

### ACT 4: THE ALBUM (7:30 - 9:30)

**ALEX:** The album was written and recorded in six weeks. How?

**COLE:** We had a tour ending in March 2026. We had a year off before the next tour. The first week after the tour ended, I went home and wrote the twelve songs. The next five weeks, we recorded them in a basement in Pacifica with a producer who lives in town.

**ALEX:** The producer — producer credit goes to Hermes Agent.

**COLE:** That's the working name. It's a friend of ours. He's brilliant. He records the way you wish your favorite records sounded. He doesn't do anything fancy. He just does everything right.

**ALEX:** The record sounds analog. It sounds like the 90s.

**COLE:** That's the goal. We grew up on Foo Fighters. We grew up on Nirvana. We grew up on Pearl Jam. We grew up on the records that made us want to do this. We wanted to make friends with those records. Not imitate them. Just — make friends with them.

**ALEX:** "Make friends with them." That's a great phrase.

**COLE:** Cole Sterling, twenty-two, just came up with it.

### OUTRO (9:30 - 10:00)

**ALEX:** The album is *Twenty-Two*. Twelve songs. Twelve memories. It's out October 23rd on RouteNote. Cole Sterling, thank you for being here.

**COLE:** Thanks for having me.

**ALEX:** This is KCRW. I'm Alex Henderson. We'll be right back.

**[Cue music — Standing Still outro, fades up briefly, then cuts]**
