## 📰 ONE-LINER

Twelve songs. Twelve memories. One year — the year a kid became a band, and then became someone else.

## 📜 ALBUM STORY

*Twenty-Two* is a coming-of-age grunge album that traces the arc of an entire rock adolescence — fifteen to twenty-two years old — in the form of twelve short stories told in chronological order.

The album came together in the aftermath of a tour that ended not with a final show but with a missed one. Pyro Altar's front singer Cole Sterling, twenty-two himself when the tour bus pulled into his hometown on the last scheduled off-day of a six-month run, opened his front door and realized he didn't recognize the kid who used to live there. The kid was gone. The kid had been gone for years. The kid was the person he'd been pretending to be.

So Cole went back to the bedroom he grew up in, picked up the first guitar he ever owned — the one with the paint-chipped stratocaster body and the broken high-E — and started writing. Not about the breakup, not about the tour, not about the band. About the things that came before the band. The first time. The first show. The first van. The first dive bar with the stage twelve inches off the ground. The first person he loved. The mother who never missed a show. The younger version of himself who'd never heard of any of this and was just sitting in his mother's living room with three chords and a dream.

The result is *Twenty-Two*: twelve songs that aren't really about a band at all. They're about the seven years you spend becoming the person who eventually plays in a band. The years before you deserved to be heard. The years you learned how to say things that only make sense once you've heard them said back.

The album moves in a deliberate arc — from raw opener (Razor, the first guitar) to the title track in the middle (Twenty-Two, the realization) to a tender back-half (Junior, addressed to the younger self) to the full-band closer (Standing Still, the moment after the album ends, looking back at all of it). Quiet tracks live next to loud ones. The recurring harmonic motif — a G–C–D–Em progression — opens the album clean and electric, returns full-band on the title cut, gets distorted and punk on Freeway, goes acoustic on Junior, and resolves with strings on Standing Still.

Pyro Altar is a four-piece from the California coast. Cole Sterling (vocals, guitar), River Holloway (guitar), Mace Voss (bass), and Ash Renner (drums) recorded *Twenty-Two* with producer Hermes Agent over six weeks in the spring of 2026. The album is dedicated to the memory of the younger self who never got to hear what he became.

### Long (300 words)

Pyro Altar is a four-piece grunge band from the California coast whose debut album *Twenty-Two* (2026) traces an entire adolescence in twelve songs — opening with the first guitar a kid ever owned and closing with the moment after the last tour ends, looking back at all of it.

The band started in 2016 in a garage in Pacifica, California, when Cole Sterling — then fifteen — taught himself three chords on a guitar his mother had bought him at a yard sale for nine dollars. Within a year the band had its first show: a Tuesday night open mic at a place called The Dive, where the stage was twelve inches off the ground and the crowd was nine people who were mostly there for the dollar wells. The Dive became theirs. They played the same five-song set every Tuesday for four months until the bartender finally started charging them for the PBR.

By 2019 they had a regional following and a self-recorded debut EP that sold out its first pressing in three weeks. They spent the next three years touring — every VFW hall, every house show, every festival that would have them — sleeping on couches, breaking down on the I-5, and slowly learning how to write songs that earned the right to be heard. *Twenty-Two* is the album that came out the other side of all that.

The album was written and recorded over six weeks in the spring of 2026, between the final date of the *In Your Honor* tour and the first rehearsals for the next one. It is dedicated to the younger self who never got to hear what he became.

Pyro Altar is **Cole Sterling** (vocals, guitar), **River Holloway** (guitar), **Mace Voss** (bass), and **Ash Renner** (drums). Based in Pacifica, California. They tour constantly.

## ❓ Q&A

**Q: Why is the album called *Twenty-Two*?**
A: It's the year the album is about. The year the tour ended. The year the singer realized he didn't know who he was without the band. The youngest age at which the album still makes sense.

**Q: Where did the album come from?**
A: The week after the final date of the *In Your Honor* tour. Cole went back to his mother's house, picked up the first guitar he ever owned, and started writing. Three weeks later there were twelve songs.

**Q: Who is Cole Sterling?**
A: Cole Sterling is the front singer of Pyro Altar. He grew up in Pacifica, learned three chords at fifteen, and has been in the band ever since. *Twenty-Two* is his first album.

**Q: Is this a concept album?**
A: Sort of. Every track is a different memory from ages 15-22, in chronological order. But the album isn't about a narrative — it's about a feeling. The feeling of looking back at the years that made you.

**Q: Why Foo Fighters? Why Nirvana?**
A: Those are the records Cole played in the basement when he was sixteen. The records that made him want to do this. The records Pyro Altar is trying to make friends with.

**Q: What's the recurring motif?**
A: G–C–D–Em. It opens the album clean on Razor, returns full-band on the title track, gets distorted and punk on Freeway, goes acoustic on Junior, and resolves with strings on Standing Still. The same four chords, told five different ways across twelve years.

**Q: What's next?**
A: Touring. The album is the first Pyro Altar record. The next one is already being written.

**Q: Where can I stream the album?**
A: Spotify, Apple Music, YouTube Music, Amazon Music, Tidal, and 150+ other platforms via RouteNote distribution. (Distribution link coming soon.)
